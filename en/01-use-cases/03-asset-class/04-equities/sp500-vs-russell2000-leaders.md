## Question

> Compare and analyze the performance of the S&P 500 and Russell 2000 from January 2026 to today. Then identify the five best-performing Russell 2000 constituents and explain why their share prices rose.

## Research objective

Comparing the two indexes alone tells us whether large caps or small caps were relatively stronger, but not what actually drove the small-cap result. Looking only at the top individual stocks has the opposite problem: it can miss whether those winners reflect a broader size rotation or a few idiosyncratic events.

The question therefore creates a narrowing research sequence:

**index-level relative performance → leaders inside the small-cap universe → company-specific catalysts.**

## Research workflow

### 1. Compare the indexes over the same period

Measure S&P 500 and Russell 2000 performance using the same start date and end date. This establishes whether the market experienced relative large-cap or small-cap strength before any company-level explanation is introduced.

### 2. Rank the strongest Russell 2000 constituents

Identify the top five performers inside the small-cap benchmark. This shows which companies actually contributed the most extreme upside within the universe rather than assuming the index move was evenly distributed.

### 3. Explain each leader's move

Investigate company-specific drivers such as:

- earnings and guidance;
- product or contract announcements;
- M&A;
- industry or thematic exposure;
- other material company catalysts.

The goal is to determine whether the top five share a common driver or rose for unrelated reasons.

### 4. Reconnect the company evidence to the index comparison

If several leaders are tied to the same industry or factor, the small-cap move may reflect a broader theme within the index. If the reasons are highly idiosyncratic, the strongest stocks may say less about the overall Russell 2000 regime.

## How to use the workflow

The value of the research pattern is that index comparison and stock screening are not treated as separate exercises.

Start with the size rotation, identify where the returns came from, then test whether the leaders share a theme. The same pattern can be reused for:

- growth versus value;
- US versus Japan;
- large caps versus mid/small caps;
- sector index versus broad benchmark.

## What this use case demonstrates

This page demonstrates the reusable analytical sequence: **benchmark comparison → leader extraction → catalyst analysis → interpretation of breadth**.
