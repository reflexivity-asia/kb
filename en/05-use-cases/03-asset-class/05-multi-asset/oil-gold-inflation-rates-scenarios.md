## Five-year monthly-change correlations

Using 59 monthly observations over the past five years, the source reports:

| Relationship | Correlation |
|---|---:|
| Gold vs US 10Y yield | -0.333 |
| Oil vs US 10Y yield | +0.268 |
| Oil vs gold | -0.152 |
| Oil vs CPI | -0.060 |
| Gold vs CPI | -0.038 |

The source interprets these as weak-to-moderate regime relationships rather than stable causal laws. Gold was more clearly inversely related to long-term yields, while oil had a modest positive relationship with yields. Same-month CPI correlations were weak, and the analysis notes that commodity prices can move ahead of reported inflation.

## Four illustrative scenarios for the next 6–12 months

A prediction-market snapshot cited in the source placed the current environment mainly in an overheating/reflation regime, with a smaller soft-landing case. The ranges below are **illustrative scenario outputs from the source**, not price targets or probabilities generated by Reflexivity.

| Scenario | Source macro premise | Oil implication | Gold implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overheating / reflation (~68%) | CPI stays around the 3% range; US 10Y remains above 5% | Firm demand and geopolitical premium; roughly $95–105 | High real yields restrain upside; roughly $4,000–4,400 |
| Soft landing (~28%) | CPI cools toward the 2% range; US 10Y moves toward the 4% range | Neutral to slightly softer as demand stays adequate but supply improves | Lower real yields support a renewed rise; roughly $4,400–4,800 |
| Recession / risk-off tail | Demand weakens sharply; cuts are front-loaded; US 10Y falls into the 3% range | Could fall below roughly $60–70 if demand breaks | Safe-haven demand supports a sharp rise, though initial liquidation is possible |
| Stagflation (low probability, ~1%) | CPI moves above 4%, rates stay high, and growth slows | Supply shock can create another spike | Stronger inflation-hedge environment |

## What the workflow connects

The analysis combines WTI, spot gold, CPI, US 10-year yields, monthly-change correlations, macro events, news, and scenario framing. The useful step is to avoid treating oil and gold as one undifferentiated “commodity” trade: their sensitivities to growth, inflation, real yields, geopolitics, and risk aversion can point in different directions.

## Limitations

- The correlation sample uses 59 monthly observations and can change materially across regimes.
- Rate and CPI relationships use monthly changes/differences, while oil and gold use price returns; same-month correlation does not establish causality.
- Gold uses spot XAU/USD; the source avoided a longer gold-futures series because contract-roll discontinuities could distort the comparison.
- WTI uses the NYMEX front-month series.
- Scenario ranges and percentages are dated source assumptions, not forecasts or investment recommendations.
