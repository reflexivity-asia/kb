# Turn an EUR/USD Investment View into a Systematic Checklist

**Author:** Reflexivity Research  
**Primary asset:** FX (EUR/USD)  
**Intended users:** FX PMs, Macro PMs, Multi-Asset Investors  
**Analysis type:** Macro analysis, investment decision framework, scenario analysis

> This page preserves the research logic and dated market observations from the source while incorporating the reasoning bridges validated in the reviewed Japanese edition. Figures and market conditions are historical snapshots from the original research, not current investment advice.

## Research objective

The source does not ask Reflexivity to simply “research EUR/USD.” Instead, it turns the questions a PM wants to revisit into a repeatable checklist so that the same decision process can be updated over time. The source research was last updated on **August 28, 2025**.

An FX view can become distorted if it is anchored to a single indicator. This checklist therefore starts with positioning to understand how the market is already leaning, then moves through relative macro fundamentals, rate differentials, valuation, risk sentiment, central-bank policy, and technicals. Only after those layers are checked does the analysis consolidate them into scenarios.

The sequence is designed to answer a progression of questions: **How is the market positioned? → Do relative fundamentals support that direction? → Do rates and policy explain the price difference? → Do valuation and technicals contradict it? → Under what scenario should the view change?**

## I. Positioning

Positioning comes first because the same macro surprise can produce a different market reaction when investors are already heavily tilted in one direction.

- CFTC EUR Futures Net: **-15,000 contracts (net short)**
- One-month change: **-2,000 contracts**
- Historical percentile: approximately **25th percentile**
- Source interpretation: speculative positioning was bearish EUR, with shorts increasing
- Explicit ECB/Fed FX intervention or USD targeting was assessed as limited

## II. Economic fundamentals

After identifying the positioning bias, the next question is whether relative economic momentum supports it. Positioning alone does not tell us whether the move is sustainable.

### PMI differential

- EU Manufacturing PMI **46.0** vs US **48.5** — a -2.5 spread favoring USD
- EU Services PMI **51.5** vs US **54.0** — also a -2.5 spread

### Labor and inflation

The source compares unemployment, employment, and core inflation as a **relative US-versus-Europe momentum** question rather than treating any single release as decisive.

## III. Rate differential

Once the relative growth and inflation picture is established, the analysis asks how those differences are reflected in monetary policy and rates. For FX, relative yields can feed directly into holding costs and cross-border capital flows.

- German 10Y Bund: **2.69%**
- US 10Y Treasury: **4.24%**
- US-DE spread: **+1.54%**
- US real rate: **1.7%**
- EU real rate: **-1.0%**
- Real-rate spread: **+2.7%**, assessed in the source as strong USD support

### Rate-differential regression

- R-squared: **0.349**
- Beta: **-0.1125**
- Model-predicted EUR/USD: **1.1270**
- Gap versus the then-current spot rate: **+0.0369**

The source interprets this as EUR trading roughly 3.7 cents rich relative to the rate-differential model.

## IV. Valuation and terms of trade

Rates alone can confuse a short-term policy gap with long-term fair value. The next layer therefore looks at REER and energy-import conditions to assess whether the spot level appears structurally rich or cheap on a different basis.

- EUR REER was assessed as broadly near fair value versus trading partners
- Brent at **$68.05** was assessed as neutral to mildly supportive for EUR through energy-import costs

## V. Risk sentiment and volatility

The analysis then checks whether the direction implied by macro and rates is also consistent with broader risk sentiment and USD behavior.

- EUR/USD 1M implied volatility: **7.6%**, around the 40th historical percentile
- VIX: **16.9**
- 30-day correlation with gold: **+0.372**
- DXY: **98.13**
- EUR/USD vs DXY correlation: **-0.916**

## VI. Central-bank policy

The source characterizes the Fed as data-dependent but relatively hawkish and the ECB as more accommodative, making **policy divergence a USD-supportive factor**.

At this stage, the purpose is to test whether the growth and rate differentials identified above are temporary market noise or consistent with a more persistent policy direction.

## VII. Technical analysis

Finally, price itself is checked to see whether the fundamental direction aligns with the current trend and nearby support/resistance. A fundamental thesis and an entry level are separate questions.

- EUR/USD spot: **1.1639**
- 20D MA: **1.1632**
- 50D MA: **1.1655**
- Immediate support: **1.1405**
- Major resistance: **1.1806**

## VIII. Scenario analysis

Rather than collapsing all evidence into a single directional forecast, the final step states which assumptions would need to change for the investment view to change.

| Scenario | Target | Probability in source | Main catalyst |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| EUR Bull | 1.1800–1.1900 | 25% | More hawkish ECB, US recession, weaker USD safe-haven demand, etc. |
| EUR Bear | 1.1200–1.1300 | 60% | EU recession, Fed higher-for-longer, USD safe-haven demand |
| Base | 1.1400–1.1600 | 15% | Gradual EUR weakness driven by policy divergence |

## Source conclusion

At the time of the original research, the output characterized the directional view as **Bearish EUR**, with high conviction over a three-to-six-month horizon. The supporting factors were the rate differential, stronger relative US economic momentum, EUR richness versus the regression model, policy divergence, technicals, and risk sentiment.

The source also contained a specific position size, stop, target, and trade recommendation. Those were dated research outputs from **August 28, 2025** and are not presented here as current investment advice.

## How to use this workflow

The value of the checklist is not that it should always produce the same conclusion. Re-running the same sequence makes it easier to see **what changed since the previous review, which supporting factor broke first, and whether scenario weights should move**.

## What this use case demonstrates

This example shows a reusable research process rather than only a final call: the starting question, data layers, supporting and contradictory evidence, explicit assumptions, scenario framing, and the conditions that would change the view.
