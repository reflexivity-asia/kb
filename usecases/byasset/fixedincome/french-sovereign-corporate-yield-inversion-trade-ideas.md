# Build Relative-Value Trades from French Sovereign-Credit Stress

**Author:** Reflexivity Research  
**Primary assets:** Fixed Income, FX, Cross-Asset  
**Intended users:** Fixed Income PMs, Macro PMs, Multi-Asset Investors  
**Analysis type:** Relative value, trade construction, scenario analysis

> This page preserves the underlying Reflexivity research and its dated market observations. The trades and levels below are historical research outputs, not current recommendations.

## Starting point

The source begins with an unusual relationship between French sovereign yields and high-quality French corporate yields, alongside elevated political risk. The research question is not only whether the dislocation is meaningful, but **how that view could be expressed across different markets**.

### Market conditions in the source

- France 10Y government yield: **3.507%**
- Germany 10Y government yield: **2.714%**, a **79 bp** France-Germany spread
- Greece 10Y government yield: **3.397%**; the source described France as yielding **11 bp more than Greece**, an unusual inversion
- Italy 10Y government yield: **3.548%**, only about **4 bp** above France
- EUR/USD: **1.1734**
- The analysis starts from the premise that high-quality French corporate yields had inverted relative to the sovereign curve

## Four fixed-income expressions

### 1. Long high-quality French corporates / short French government bonds

The source proposes buying same-maturity bonds from high-quality issuers such as L'Oréal, Airbus, and AXA while shorting French government bonds. It highlights the 5–10 year area as a relatively practical expression of the corporate-sovereign yield inversion while considering liquidity.

### 2. France-Germany sovereign spread widening

Short France 10Y and long Germany 10Y. The source considers a widening from 79 bp toward roughly 100–120 bp in a stress scenario while explicitly identifying **ECB intervention or a rapid political resolution** as key risks.

### 3. French curve steepening

Short France 10Y and long 2Y. The hypothesis is that political risk premia concentrate further out the curve. The source uses a move from roughly 140 bp in the 2s10s slope toward more than 180 bp as an illustrative scenario.

### 4. Reversal of peripheral convergence

Short French government bonds and long Greek government bonds. The source treats the France-Greece yield inversion as a historically unusual condition and discusses duration matching and stop conditions if the relationship moves the other way.

## Cross-asset extensions

### 5. Short EUR versus safer currencies

EUR/USD, EUR/CHF, and EUR/JPY shorts are presented as possible expressions, including options structures to limit downside risk.

### 6. French equity sector rotation

Long defensives such as utilities and staples, short more cyclical or financial exposures. The source also considers holding a company such as L'Oréal through both equity and credit, or shorting French banks while hedging broad market beta with the CAC 40.

### 7. European credit-quality dispersion

Long German and Dutch corporate credit versus French corporate credit. The hypothesis is that sovereign stress could spill into corporate spreads with a lag.

## Relative-value and volatility extensions

8. **Volatility:** long EUR/USD and French sovereign volatility, with three-to-six-month straddles as an example.  
9. **Futures/cash basis:** look for temporary dislocations between French government-bond futures and cash bonds.  
10. **Cross-currency basis:** express a France-driven EUR funding premium through EUR/USD cross-currency basis or FX forwards.

## Risk management

The source states failure conditions alongside the trade ideas:

- ECB bond purchases or other intervention
- rapid political resolution and spread compression
- liquidity constraints in individual French corporate bonds
- a broader European crisis that breaks the assumed relationship between France and the intended “safe” hedge markets

## Source's preferred expression

The source ultimately prioritizes **long 5–10 year high-quality French corporate bonds / short French government bonds** as the expression most directly linked to the central dislocation.

It also includes a six-month horizon and an illustrative 2–3:1 risk/reward estimate. Those are dated research outputs from the original analysis and should not be read as a current recommendation or current executable level.

## What this use case demonstrates

This example shows how to move from an observed sovereign-credit anomaly to a menu of trade expressions across rates, credit, FX, equities, volatility, and basis markets while preserving explicit failure conditions rather than stopping at a market narrative.
