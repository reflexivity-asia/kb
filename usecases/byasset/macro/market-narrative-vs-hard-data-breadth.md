# Test Whether a Market Narrative Is Supported by Broad Hard Data

**Author:** Reflexivity Research  
**Primary assets:** Macro, Equities, Fixed Income, Cross-Asset  
**Intended users:** CIOs, Macro PMs, Multi-Asset PMs, Strategists  
**Analysis type:** Narrative analysis, cross-asset analysis, market monitoring

> This page preserves a dated Reflexivity research output. The purpose is to show how a market narrative can be tested against multiple evidence sets rather than accepted from headlines alone.

## Research question

**Is the narrative that “the US economy is strong” supported by broad evidence, or is it being carried by a smaller number of strong headline observations?**

The source combines roughly 60 days of macro-related news, Fed-related information, prediction-market pricing, equity-theme leadership, and hard economic data into one evidence table.

## Source conclusion

The source judges the breadth of support as **limited**. Hard data are mixed, prediction markets reflect inflation and additional-tightening risk rather than a clean growth story, and equity-market leadership remains concentrated.

## Three major divergences

### 1. Employment: headline strength versus breadth

- August payrolls: **162k**, versus a **56k** market expectation
- ADP private payrolls: **38k**

The headline number looks strong, but broader labor evidence is less consistent with that message.

### 2. Policy outlook: pricing versus a simple disinflation story

- Policy hold: **50.5%**
- 25 bp hike: **49.5%**
- Cut: about **0.5%**
- Core PCE: **3.3%**

If the dominant story were simply “strong growth with clean disinflation,” easier-policy pricing might be expected to strengthen. Instead, the source finds markets still assigning substantial probability to additional tightening.

### 3. Equity leadership: index strength versus narrow participation

- One-month spread between the best- and worst-performing themes: about **48 percentage points** (+33.4% to -14.7%)
- **10 themes** in the research set were negative
- S&P 500: **7,718.6**, close to its 52-week high of 7,799

The index was near its high while internal theme performance remained highly dispersed.

## Cross-evidence scorecard

| Evidence type | Market narrative | Hard-data / market check | Source assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employment | Payroll headline strong | ADP and broader indicators softer | Limited breadth |
| Fed / prediction markets | Easing narrative | Hold and hike nearly evenly priced; core PCE 3.3% | Contradiction |
| Equity leadership | Broad rally | 48-point dispersion; 10 themes negative | Limited breadth |
| News tone | Strong economy | Inflation/hike-risk headlines much more common | Cautious |
| Manufacturing / growth | Expansion continues | ISM new orders 56.0 → 53.7; retail sales softer | Slowing |
| Year-end growth regime | Soft landing | Soft landing 55%; overheating 42.5% | View remains split |

## Additional signals in the source

- Across 30 macro-related headlines, stories emphasizing rate-hike or inflation risk outnumbered strong-data headlines by roughly **3.5 to 1**.
- ISM New Orders moved from **56.0 to 53.7**.
- Prediction-market probabilities cited: Soft Landing **55%**, Overheating **42.5%**.
- Initial jobless claims of **206k** and unemployment at **4.1%** remained evidence supporting resilience.

## Limitations

- The source could not retrieve a complete set of primary Fed documents for the period and used some economic-calendar and news material as a proxy.
- Equity-theme names were anonymized, so breadth was measured through return dispersion rather than named sectors.
- News-tone counts are keyword-based headline counts, not a calibrated sentiment model.
- Surprise comparisons are concentrated in the most recent available releases because of data-coverage limits.

## How to use the workflow

The key discipline is to avoid judging “the market narrative” from newsflow alone. The same question should be checked against **macro data, rates, market internals, and prediction-market pricing**.

The result may be confirmation, contradiction, or narrow support. The objective is to make those disagreements visible rather than compress them into a single story.

## What this use case demonstrates

This example shows how to measure narrative breadth across independent evidence sets and identify when a strong headline environment is not matched by equally broad market or economic confirmation.
