# Prioritize the Week's US Market Events by Likely Impact

**Author:** QUICK Inc.  
**Provided:** September 8, 2026  
**Primary assets:** Macro, Equities, Fixed Income, FX  
**Intended users:** Wealth Management / RIA, Long-only Asset Managers, Hedge Fund Tier 2

> This page is based on a use case provided by QUICK Inc. Figures, consensus estimates, and event timing are source-date snapshots. The value of the example is the prioritization workflow rather than the historical calendar itself.

> List the US economic releases and other events this week that have the highest potential to affect markets.

## Research objective

An economic calendar can contain dozens of events, but they do not deserve equal attention. The goal here is not to reproduce a calendar. It is to prioritize events by **how likely they are to change monetary-policy expectations and then transmit into rates, the dollar, and equities**.

The source uses a hierarchy:

1. inflation releases that can directly change the policy debate;
2. labor and inflation-expectation data that can confirm or challenge that view;
3. Treasury supply, housing, energy, and positioning data that matter more directly for specific markets or sectors.

That turns a list of dates into a research plan for allocating attention during the week.

## Highest priority: inflation data

The source places CPI and PPI at the top because they can affect Fed expectations and reprice several asset classes at once.

| Date / time in source (JST) | Release | Source consensus | Why it matters |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Sep. 11, 21:30 | August CPI MoM | +0.4% | Highest-priority release of the week |
| Sep. 11, 21:30 | August CPI YoY | +3.4% | Inflation trend |
| Sep. 11, 21:30 | August core CPI YoY | +2.4% | Direct input into policy expectations |
| Sep. 10, 21:30 | August PPI MoM | +0.3% | Upstream inflation signal |
| Sep. 10, 21:30 | August core PPI MoM | +0.3% | Underlying producer-price trend |

## Next priority: labor and consumer expectations

Inflation alone does not determine the policy interpretation. A sharp deterioration in employment could change the implications of a strong price print, so the next tier checks whether the labor market and inflation expectations confirm or complicate the inflation signal.

- **Initial jobless claims:** Sep. 10, 21:30 JST; source consensus 205k, with continuing claims also monitored.
- **University of Michigan consumer sentiment and inflation expectations:** Sep. 11, 23:00 JST.
- **NFIB Small Business Optimism:** Sep. 8, 19:00 JST; source consensus 99.3.

## Follow-up tier: market-specific supply and sector signals

These events are still useful, but the source treats them more as inputs into particular markets than as the week's dominant policy driver.

- **Treasury auctions:** 3Y on Sep. 8 and 10Y on Sep. 9; demand can affect longer-term yields.
- **Consumer credit and wholesale inventories:** additional activity and demand signals.
- **Housing:** existing-home sales and mortgage-market data.
- **Fiscal balance:** August budget data.
- **Energy:** EIA crude inventories, EIA short-term outlook, OPEC and IEA reports, natural-gas inventories.
- **Positioning / supply:** CFTC speculative positions and the Baker Hughes rig count toward week-end.

## Source summary

The source's hierarchy puts **August CPI** first, followed by **PPI / jobless claims** and the **Michigan survey**. The central reasoning is that inflation-sensitive releases can change Fed expectations and therefore raise volatility simultaneously across equities, rates, and FX, while the lower tiers are more targeted to specific markets or sectors.

## How to use the workflow

The key weekly question is not “how many events are on the calendar?” but **which events update the same common policy driver**.

After each release, compare the result not only with consensus but also with:

- changes in policy pricing;
- Treasury yields;
- the dollar;
- equity reaction.

If the market reaction differs from the event's pre-assigned importance, that itself becomes useful information for the next week's prioritization.

## What this use case demonstrates

This example converts a weekly macro calendar into a ranked monitoring plan: policy-moving events first, confirming or contradictory data second, and market-specific supply or sector inputs third.
