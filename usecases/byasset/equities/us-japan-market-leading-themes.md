# Compare the Themes Leading US and Japanese Equities

**Author:** QUICK  
**Provided:** May 15, 2026  
**Primary assets:** Equities, Multi-Asset

> This page is based on a QUICK use case. Customer and recipient information, signatures, and private Research URLs have been removed while preserving the source question, evidence, analytical sequence, and limitations as closely as possible. Figures and market conditions are historical snapshots from the source period.

> Are the themes behind the leading companies in the US equity market similar to the themes leading the Japanese market? If there are differences, what are they? Analyze the past three months of performance.

## Research design

The source compares roughly **February 14 to May 14, 2026** and starts by putting US and Japanese market leaders on the same performance frame.

Reference benchmarks in the source:
- S&P 500: **+8.2%**
- Nikkei 225: **+11.4%**
- USD/JPY: **+2.8%**, indicating yen depreciation over the period

The sequence is deliberate: first normalize the market leaders to identify what drove returns, then ask whether the same themes exist in both markets and, where they do, whether the same part of the value chain is actually leading.

## What looked similar

### AI and semiconductors were common leadership themes

The source's strongest commonality is the AI-demand chain. US leadership appeared more concentrated in chip design, cloud platforms, and data-center monetization, while Japan participated through semiconductor equipment, hardware, investment exposure, and automation.

### Semiconductor equipment linked both markets

The source points to strong US semiconductor-equipment exposure through SMH and to Tokyo Electron in Japan. The theme exists in both markets even though the magnitude of the returns differed materially.

## What looked different

### Industrial automation was a more important Japanese leadership theme

Japanese companies such as Keyence, Yaskawa, Fanuc, and Mitsubishi Electric were prominent in the source's automation basket. The comparable US automation names rose as well, but the theme was less dominant at the broad US index level.

### Cloud / digital platforms were primarily a US expression

The source distinguishes Amazon, Alphabet, and Microsoft as large global cloud / AI platform companies without direct Japanese equivalents of the same scale and business model. SoftBank's role in the source is different: investment and AI exposure rather than direct cloud-platform monetization.

### Autos and globally exposed manufacturing diverged

Toyota materially underperformed in the source period. The underlying QUICK analysis attributed part of that weakness to tariff/cost pressure and intensifying EV competition in China, while noting that globally exposed Japanese manufacturers can be more directly affected by trade, FX, and overseas competitive conditions. These are source-period attributions, not a general current claim about the sector.

## Source synthesis

The broad AI / semiconductor direction was common to both markets, but the **economic layer capturing the return differed**:

- US leadership: software, cloud platforms, chip design, direct AI monetization;
- Japan leadership: equipment, sensors, industrial automation, and hardware-enabling businesses.

The source therefore describes both “resonance” at the high-level theme and a structural “fault line” in where the value chain is represented in public markets.

## Limitations

- Japanese equity performance is calculated in JPY; the source notes USD/JPY moved +2.8%, so USD-based returns would differ.
- Sector/theme classification uses representative companies rather than official TOPIX industry indexes.
- Some company-specific causal explanations rely on source news and available data rather than a complete earnings decomposition.
- The source notes that some Japanese automation stocks may have reflected expectations for future AI / recovery rather than contemporaneous profit strength.

## What this use case demonstrates

This workflow compares two equity markets at the theme level without assuming that a shared headline theme implies the same investable exposure. It moves from normalized performance to common themes, value-chain differences, company-level evidence, and the next fundamental questions to investigate.
